# Ch12 — Bluffers and Market Manipulation

**Source:** Larry Harris, *Trading and Exchanges* (2003), Chapter 12.

## Purpose
How manipulation works: bluffers create false trading signals to fool
uninformed traders into offering liquidity on bad terms.

## Why bluffing works
- Uninformed traders infer information from **order flow and price
  action**. A bluffer fakes that evidence: placing a large visible
  order or a burst of market orders mimics informed trading.
- Because price adjustment depends on the *composition* of flow
  (informed vs. uninformed), and that composition is unobservable,
  bluffed flow can move prices exactly like real informed flow.
- The bluffer then exits quietly at the improved price.

## Manipulation techniques
1. **Wash trading**: trading with yourself (or a confederate) to
   create false volume and activity signals.
2. **Spoofing / layering**: posting large non-bona-fide orders at
   prices you never intend to fill, to fake demand/supply and push
   the price; canceling once the real orders fill. (Now explicitly
   illegal under Dodd-Frank.)
3. **Market-order bluffing**: firing market orders to move price,
   then reversing.
4. **Pumping and dumping**: spreading false information (rumor
   campaigns) to raise price, then selling into the rise.
5. **Painting the tape**: executing trades at improving prices to
   create the appearance of momentum.
6. **Runs and corners** (historic): cornering supply to squeeze
   shorts.

## Who gets hurt
- Uninformed traders who infer value from bluffed signals, plus
  liquidity suppliers caught on the wrong side of a manufactured move.
- Manipulation is hard to distinguish from legitimate trading —
  which is why detection (surveillance of quote/trade patterns,
  cancel-to-trade ratios) is an arms race.

## Key takeaways
- Bluffing is profitable only when there are **uninformed traders
  willing to extrapolate from order flow** — the same population that
  makes markets liquid.
- Trading *against* apparent momentum without independent value
  information is how you get manipulated; price action alone cannot
  distinguish bluffs from information.
- Regulation targets the *non-bona-fide* character (orders submitted
  without intent to trade); for practitioners, high cancel rates and
  size-without-fill patterns are the red flags to avoid following.
