# Ch08 — Traders (Who Trades, and Why)

**Source:** Larry Harris, *Trading and Exchanges* (2003), Chapter 8.

## Purpose
A taxonomy of traders by their information and motivation, and how
each type's behavior shapes prices, spreads, and market design.

## The trader types
1. **Informed traders** — trade on private information about value.
   They profit at the expense of everyone they trade with; their
   activity pushes prices toward fundamental value (price discovery)
   but widens spreads (adverse selection).
2. **Value traders / arbitrageurs** — trade on *public* information,
   believing prices deviate from fundamental value. They are the
   market's self-correcting mechanism.
3. **News traders** — react to public announcements; a subclass of
   value traders with shorter horizons.
4. **Technical traders** — trade on price patterns and volume; can be
   self-fulfilling or destabilizing.
5. **Uninformed (utilitarian) traders** — hedge, rebalance, exchange
   assets; their liquidity demand is the "noise" that makes markets
   function (informed traders need someone to trade against).
6. **Order anticipators** — detect and trade ahead of large uninformed
   order flow.
7. **Bluffers** — manipulate prices to fool others.
8. **Gamblers** — trade for entertainment, usually on stale
   information.

## Why the taxonomy matters
- The **mix of traders** determines market quality: more informed
  traders → more informative prices but wider spreads; more uninformed
  flow → narrower spreads but noisier prices.
- Each trader type needs different market structures: informed traders
  want speed and anonymity; uninformed patient traders want low cost
  and time priority; gamblers want entertainment.
- **Zero-sum logic**: aggregate profits of informed traders equal
  aggregate losses of uninformed traders (minus transaction costs).
  Markets are transfer mechanisms, not money printers.

## Key takeaways
- Ask *who is my counterparty* before trading: if you cannot identify
  why they trade, assume they are better informed than you.
- The market works because uninformed traders provide the liquidity
  that informed traders monetize — both groups are essential, and
  neither can survive without the other.
- Design decisions (tick size, speed, transparency, fees) shift the
  trader mix, which is why microstructure regulation is so contested.
