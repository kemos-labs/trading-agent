# Ch16 — Value Traders

**Source:** Larry Harris, *Trading and Exchanges* (2003), Chapter 16.

## Purpose
Value traders — the market's correction mechanism: how they estimate
fundamental value, why they trade cautiously, and what limits their
ability to discipline prices.

## Who they are
- Value traders hold no private information; they believe prices
  deviate from *fundamental value* that anyone could estimate from
  public information (cash flows, balance sheets, earnings power).
- Types: long-term investors, value investors, some technical traders,
  and (short-horizon) the arbitrageurs of Ch17.
- They buy when price < value, sell/short when price > value — trading
  *against* the crowd.

## Why they are cautious
- **Value uncertainty**: if value is hard to estimate (new
  technologies, no earnings), traders won't act until the mispricing
  is large — which is exactly why bubbles form in unproven
  technology stocks.
- **Confidence problem**: markets aggregate information well, so a
  value trader must always wonder why they know better than everyone
  else. Doubt delays action.
- **Timing problem**: prices can stay wrong longer than the trader can
  stay solvent — being early is being wrong; margin constraints can
  force liquidation before the correction.

## Their role
- Value traders supply the **resiliency** of markets: after
  uninformed shocks push prices away from value, value traders bring
  them back — the mean-reversion force that separates transitory from
  fundamental volatility.
- Their expected profits are the incentive for the research that keeps
  prices informative; without them, prices drift (see Ch10).

## Key takeaways
- Value trading is a **conviction-weighted discipline**: position
  sizing should reflect both the size of the mispricing *and* the
  uncertainty of your value estimate.
- The practical question is not "is it cheap?" but "can I survive
  until it revalues?" — horizon, leverage, and drawdown tolerance
  decide whether a mispricing is tradeable.
- Because value traders only act on large deviations, prices can
  become *more* informative about the *consensus*, not about value —
  respect the market's aggregation power before betting against it.
