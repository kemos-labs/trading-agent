# Ch09 — Good Markets (Liquidity, Volatility, and Transparency)

**Source:** Larry Harris, *Trading and Exchanges* (2003), Chapter 9.

## Purpose
Defines market quality: what makes a market "good," how to measure
it, and why liquidity, volatility, and transparency interact.

## The dimensions of market quality
1. **Liquidity** — the ability to trade quickly at low cost without
   moving price. Components:
   - **Width**: bid/ask spread (cost of immediacy).
   - **Depth**: size available at quoted prices.
   - **Resiliency**: how fast prices recover after a trade.
   - **Immediacy**: how quickly orders execute.
2. **Volatility** — two kinds:
   - **Fundamental volatility**: price changes due to new information
     about value (permanent).
   - **Transitory volatility**: price changes due to order-flow
     imbalances and liquidity demand (reverting).
3. **Transparency** — the availability of pre-trade (quotes, book) and
   post-trade (trades, volume) information. Transparency helps
   price discovery but can hurt liquidity suppliers who want to hide
   size.

## The central trade-offs
- More liquidity → lower transaction costs → more trading → better
  price discovery (a virtuous cycle, the **order flow externality**).
- Transparency vs. liquidity: full display lets informed traders pick
  off resting limit orders, so traders hide size and markets become
  less deep at the touch.
- Volatility and liquidity are linked: when uncertainty rises,
  liquidity suppliers withdraw and transitory volatility spikes —
  spreads widen exactly when traders need to trade.

## Measurement
- Spreads (quoted, effective), depth at the best prices, Amihud
  illiquidity (|return|/volume), and price-impact regressions are the
  standard liquidity metrics.
- Good markets are judged by *cost of trading* — the total of spread,
  impact, and delay — not by price level.

## Key takeaways
- A "good market" is one with **low transaction costs, informative
  prices, and resilient liquidity**; these goals sometimes conflict.
- Transitory volatility is the price of liquidity provision — it is
  not evidence of market failure.
- For any trading strategy, measure the four liquidity dimensions
  before committing capital: width, depth, resiliency, and immediacy
  jointly determine achievable returns.
