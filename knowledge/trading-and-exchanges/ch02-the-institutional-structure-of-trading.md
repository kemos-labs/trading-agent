# Ch02 — The Institutional Structure of Trading

**Source:** Larry Harris, *Trading and Exchanges* (2003), Chapter 2.

## Purpose
Explains where trades happen: the institutional landscape of exchanges,
OTC markets, and the clearing/settlement systems that stand behind every
trade.

## Where trading occurs
- **Exchanges**: centralized facilities with organized rules — members
  trade on a floor or via electronic systems; orders are matched under
  defined precedence rules. Listing (for equities) or listing/clearing
  (for futures) ties the contract to the venue.
- **OTC markets**: negotiated, bilateral trading between dealers and
  clients (bonds, swaps, FX). No central matching; dealers quote to
  clients who shop among them.
- **Third markets**: OTC trading of exchange-listed securities (the
  "third market" for listed stocks, e.g. third-market dealers matching
  orders in NYSE stocks off the floor).

## Clearing and settlement
- **Clearing**: netting obligations and confirming trades. A
  **clearinghouse** interposes itself between buyer and seller, becoming
  the buyer to every seller and the seller to every buyer — this
  novation converts bilateral credit risk into centralized, margined
  risk.
- **Settlement**: the actual transfer of cash and securities (T+1/T+2
  in modern markets; T+5 historically in the U.S.).
- **Margin** systems (futures especially) require mark-to-market daily,
  with variation margin covering losses so that defaults cannot
  cascade.
- **Failures to settle** are a cost borne by counterparties; markets
  police this via credit standards, clearinghouse membership
  requirements, and capital adequacy rules.

## Key roles
- **Depositories** hold securities in electronic book-entry form,
  eliminating physical certificate transfer.
- **Exchanges and regulators** cooperate on surveillance (detecting
  manipulation, insider trading) and on enforcing fair access.
- The clearinghouse's guarantee function is what lets a fragmented,
  trust-limited market operate: traders trade with the clearinghouse,
  not with anonymous strangers.

## Key takeaways
- A trade's value depends on the **institutional infrastructure** behind
  it: clearing, settlement, and credit risk are real transaction costs
  even when invisible to the trader.
- Different instruments map to different institutional homes (stocks →
  exchanges + depositories; bonds → OTC dealer networks; futures →
  exchange clearinghouses with daily margining).
- When comparing market structures, always ask *who stands behind the
  trade* — the answer determines counterparty risk, and therefore what
  spreads and margins are required.
