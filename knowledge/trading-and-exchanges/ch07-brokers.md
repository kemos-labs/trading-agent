# Ch07 — Brokers

**Source:** Larry Harris, *Trading and Exchanges* (2003), Chapter 7.

## Purpose
The economics of brokerage: why brokers exist, how they add value, and
the agency conflicts that plague the broker–client relationship.

## Why brokers add value
1. **Access**: brokers are exchange members; clients trade through
   them.
2. **Expertise**: brokers know how to work orders (split, time,
   route) to minimize transaction costs.
3. **Capital and credit**: brokers extend credit (margin) and
   guarantee settlement for clients.
4. **Search**: brokers find counterparties for large/illiquid orders
   (upstairs market, block facilitation).
5. **Anonymity**: brokers shield client identity, reducing the
   information leakage that moves prices.

## Agency conflicts
- **Trading ahead**: a broker fills their own account before a client
  order at the same price (front-running) — prohibited but costly to
  police.
- **Payments for order flow**: brokers route orders to dealers who pay
  rebates; if execution quality is worse than the primary market,
  clients absorb the difference.
- **Order exposure**: brokers may reveal client orders to favored
  traders; clients get worse prices.
- **Workout discretion**: "not-held" orders give brokers discretion —
  good brokers improve price; lazy/dishonest brokers fill at
  convenience.

## The execution audit problem
- Clients cannot easily verify whether a broker obtained good prices:
  they lack the data and the norms to benchmark. This is *why* the SEC
  mandates execution-quality disclosure (Rule 11Ac1-5/6) and why
  best-execution duties exist.
- The broker's value is real but unmeasurable ex post; the industry
  therefore runs on trust, regulation, and reputation.

## Key takeaways
- Brokers are a **solution to search and access costs** but create an
  **agency problem** in return; the fees clients pay buy access and
  expertise, not loyalty.
- Prefer brokers whose incentives align: execution-quality
  transparency, no internalization conflicts, and audit-friendly
  reporting beat low headline commissions.
- When delegating order execution, specify the execution benchmark
  (arrival price, VWAP, implementation shortfall) in advance — the
  benchmark is the only way to audit the broker afterward.
