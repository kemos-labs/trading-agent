# Chapter 5 — Why Have They Become Mainstream so Quickly?

Source: Leshik & Cralle, *An Introduction to Algorithmic Trading* (2011)

## Adoption drivers

- **Technical**: Moore's Law compute (a desktop ≈ the NASA machines of
  the Apollo era), fiber-optic telecom bandwidth, maturing infrastructure
  — technologies crossing the "tipping point" together. Algos contribute
  to the bottom line across cost-performance dimensions → a "must have."
- **Conceptual / institutional**: a designer (often with the CEO's
  vision) iterates a strategy for weeks/months → programmers convert the
  spec to optimized executable software → extensive **backtesting**
  against benchmarks → rigorous pre-live testing ("does exactly what
  intended, and nothing else") → production deployment, amplified across
  thousands of stocks beyond human reach; teams refine as reality hits.
- **Immediate benefits**: cost per trader ↓, throughput ↑, self-
  documenting trade trail (control/regulatory), fewer trading errors,
  performance consistency, less emotional "burnout."
- **Strategic**: exponential trading-load growth made "more traders and
  floors" unsustainable; machines watch endlessly for a parameter
  trigger without fatigue — the "Algo Age."

## Key takeaways

1. The institutional lifecycle: idea → spec → code → backtest → stress →
   production → continuous refinement; backtesting is a gate, not a
   guarantee.
2. Adoption was an infrastructure tipping point, not a single invention.
