# Chapter 39 — Information Sources

Source: Leshik & Cralle, *An Introduction to Algorithmic Trading* (2011)

A reference list (2010 vintage; verify currency before relying on it)
of websites, journals, and blogs the authors find useful.

## Websites / data
Google Finance, Yahoo Finance, MarketWatch, tradethenews.com,
fxstreet.com, trade2win.com, seekingalpha.com, SantaFe.edu (complexity),
arXiv.org (e-print archive, econophysics).

## News / magazines
Bloomberg, CNBC (US content), Wall Street Journal (online market data),
Financial Times, Barron's (weekly). Bookmark NASDAQ, NASDAQtrader,
NYSE home sites. *Technical Analysis of Stocks and Commodities* (the
"basic bible of the individual trader"); *Automated Trader* (UK,
algo-oriented); *Futures Magazine*, *Active Trader*, *SFO Magazine*,
*Traders' Magazine* (Germany), *ActionFuture* (France); *Journal of
Trading*, *Journal of Investing*, *Technical Analyst* (UK), *Traders
Journal* (Asia).

## Blogs (a sampling; the field churns constantly)
Dr. Van Tharp, Stockbee, The Kirk Report, Toni Hansen, Timothy Sykes,
TraderMike, Wall St. Warrior, Downtown trader, and many others (the
book lists ~30).

## Advice
Follow selected stocks' fortunes (useful + entertaining); scan blog
lists occasionally to see what other traders are thinking/doing;
creative ideas often spark there. The field is fluid — new titles
appear as older ones vanish.

## Key takeaways

1. Maintain a curated, periodically refreshed reading list across news,
   data, journals, and blogs — ideas spark from cross-pollination.
2. *Technical Analysis of Stocks and Commodities* is the baseline
   periodical for the individual trader; *Automated Trader* for the
   algo angle.
