# Chapter 38 — Brief Philosophical Digression

Source: Leshik & Cralle, *An Introduction to Algorithmic Trading* (2011)

## Closing reflection

- Humankind's understanding of the world is still basic, including our
  mathematical tools. We've assumed for centuries that things are
  *linear*; the linear state is the great exception. Nonlinearity took
  center stage only mid-20th century.
- **Mandelbrot** showed there's virtually no such thing as "linear" in
  nature, quantified "roughness," and criticized the misuse of
  mathematics in finance (simplifying assumptions needed to make models
  work at all → spurious results, used because they're easy to
  calculate).
- The current tool chest, however extensive, is a "child's toy toolset"
  against the complexities and multidimensionality of real-life price
  series — "as if someone handed us a teaspoon and pointed at a
  mountain."
- If you persevere and keep turning up, the mountain may turn out to
  be made of paper. The markets may be extraordinarily bountiful to
  those with the passion, intellect, and tenacity to stay the course.
- Sign-off: "Happy and profitable trading — may all your dreams come
  true." — Jane and Edward.

## Key takeaways

1. Linear thinking and simplifying-assumption math are inadequate for
   real markets; treat all models as provisional.
2. The recurring theme of the book: humility + perseverance + local,
   short-lookback empirical work over grand theory.
