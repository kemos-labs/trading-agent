# Ch2 — Introduction to Bayesian Statistics

**Source:** Halls-Moore, *Advanced Algorithmic Trading*, Chapter 2.

## Core philosophy
**Bayesian statistics** treats probability as a *measure of belief/confidence*, not as a long-run
relative frequency. Its central operation is **updating beliefs in light of new evidence**:
we begin with a *prior* belief, combine it with observed *data/evidence* using **Bayes'
Rule**, and obtain a *posterior* belief. Crucially, a posterior can itself serve as the prior
for the next update, allowing continual rational belief adjustment as data arrives.

This contrast with **Frequentist (Classical) statistics**, where probability is the long-run
relative frequency of an event over repeated trials (e.g. the frequency of heads over many flips).
- Frequentist: tries to *eliminate* uncertainty by producing a point/density estimate.
- Bayesian: tries to *quantify* uncertainty by adjusting beliefs.
- Different rational agents may hold different priors, but as evidence diffuses, posteriors
  converge (a key property for markets).
- Danger (esp. quants): treating merely "*very unlikely*" events as *impossible* (probability 0).
  This ignores tail-risk — e.g. Barings 1995, LTCM 1998, Lehman 2008. Bayesian framing pushes
  you to assign tiny-but-nonzero prior weight to rare catastrophic outcomes.

## Bayes' Rule derivation

Starting from the definition of conditional probability:
- P(A|B) = P(A∩B) / P(B)  →  P(B)P(A|B) = P(A∩B)
- Symmetrically P(A)P(B|A) = P(B∩A) = P(A∩B).
- Equating and dividing by P(B): **P(A|B) = P(B|A)·P(A)/P(B)**.

For a parameter θ and observed data D (the inference form):
**P(θ|D) = P(D|θ)·P(θ) / P(D)**   (2.10)

Terminology:
- **P(θ)** — *prior*: strength of belief in θ before seeing D.
- **P(θ|D)** — *posterior*: refined belief after seeing D.
- **P(D|θ)** — *likelihood*: probability of the data generated by a model with parameter θ.
- **P(D)** — *evidence*: probability of data summed/integrated over all θ, weighted by how
  strongly we believe each θ (marginalising the model space).

The denominator can be written as a summation over an exhaustive partition of the sample
space: P(B) = Σ_a P(B|a)P(a), giving the alternative "sum over states" form of Bayes' rule.
Model = the machinery encoding the coin flip; parameter θ = P(H) ∈ [0,1].

## Worked example: coin-flip Bayesian updating

Repeated Bernoulli trials with true θ = 0.5 but unknown to the observer. Start with a
**uniform prior** (equal belief across every θ). Using the **Beta-Binomial conjugate model**,
each observed flip updates a Beta posterior:
- After 2 heads: mass shifts right → inclines toward "biased to heads".
- After 10/20 trials (with a few tails): density drifts back toward θ=0.5, widening uncertainty.
- After 50/500 trials: density becomes tightly concentrated near θ and narrow.
- The posterior width shrinks as data accumulate → uncertainty shrinks with sample size.
- Rational inference goes from *high uncertainty, little data* to *low uncertainty, lots of data*.

## Key takeaways / code pattern
- Conjugate Beta prior for the binomial likelihood yields a **Beta posterior** — hugely
  simplifying the algebra (topic of next chapter).
- Python: SciPy `stats.bernoulli.rvs(0.5, size=N)` generates flips; `Beta` from SciPy for the
  densities; update = accumulate heads across trial counts and re-plot the Beta density:
  - uniform prior via Beta(1,1) → after k heads and n−k tails, posterior = Beta(1+k, 1+(n−k)).
- Theore core idea (Bayes' rule as a *rational belief-updating procedure*) underpins all
  later chapters on stochastic volatility, Kalman/Hidden-Markov regimes, and ML.

## Notes
- No new invalid claims: verified Bayes' rule algebra is standard conditional-probability
  manipulation; Beta(α,β) conjugacy is the canonical reference result.
- Watch the tail-risk bias as a recurring pitfall when eliciting priors in trading models.
