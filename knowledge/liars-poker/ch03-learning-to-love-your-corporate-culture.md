# Ch03 — Learning to Love Your Corporate Culture

**Source:** Michael Lewis, *Liar's Poker* (1989), Chapter 3.

## Purpose
Explains *where the money came from*: the 1980s bond-market boom and
Salomon Brothers' dominance of it — the macroeconomic and structural
forces that made bond trading Wall Street's most profitable game.

## The two "hands stuffing the turkey"
1. **The Federal Reserve (Oct 6, 1979)**: Volcker announced money supply
   would be fixed and interest rates would float. Overnight, interest
   rates swung wildly — and bond prices move inversely, lockstep, with
   rates. Bonds went from conservative savings vehicles to speculation
   instruments. The bond market became a casino.
2. **America's borrowers**: governments, consumers, and corporations
   borrowed at an unprecedented clip (combined debt $323B in 1977 → $7T
   by 1985), and a greater share was cast as bonds rather than bank
   loans. Volume of tradeable bonds exploded.

## The toll-taker model of bond profits
- The biggest myth: bond traders make money by taking big risks. A few
  do; most act as **toll takers** — carving a tiny fraction (an eighth of
  a percentage point) out of each transaction that passes through them.
- Vonnegut's formulation (about lawyers): the magic microsecond between
  surrender and receipt — possessing the treasure briefly, taking a
  little, passing it on.
- Example: selling $50M of IBM bonds to a pension fund nets the trader
  ~1/8 point = $62,500. Then he can generate more "magic microseconds":
  buy from X, resell to Y at a better price, taking another eighth.
- Unlike stocks, bond commissions are not openly stated — so the spread
  is the invisible toll.
- Salomon's edge: knowing who the **fool** is (Buffett: "any player
  unaware of the fool in the market probably is the fool"). A fool sells
  a bond for less or buys it for more than it's worth; Salomon priced
  bonds properly.

## Why Salomon specifically
- Salomon knew more about bonds than anyone (valuation, trading, sales);
  only gap was junk bonds (Drexel's specialty, then tiny).
- Bond trading had been unfashionable and unprofitable, so competitors
  ceded it; Salomon became the best by default and then by habit.
- 1985: "man for man, the world's most profitable corporation."

## Key takeaways
- Regime changes (monetary policy) can transform a market's character —
  the 1979 Volcker shift made rates (and bond prices) the speculation
  vehicle of the decade.
- Market-making is largely toll collection: the spread, not the position,
  is the business. Scale + flow beats big directional bets.
- Knowing who the counterparty is — and whether they understand the
  value — is the trader's core information advantage.
