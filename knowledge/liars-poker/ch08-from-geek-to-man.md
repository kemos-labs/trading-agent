# Ch08 — From Geek to Man

**Source:** Michael Lewis, *Liar's Poker* (1989), Chapter 8.

## Purpose
Lewis's transfer to Salomon's **London office** (1985–88): the sales
floor as a calling business, the globalization race, and the 
Eurobond/Euromarket culture — plus the "geek to man" rite of passage.

## The geek's education in sales
- New salesmen cold-called European money managers (the Euromoney Guide
  of $50M+ institutions) — "call everyone in Paris, and smile." Pronouncing
  names wrong, getting hung up on, being treated as part of the "Salomon
  bastards."
- The role: **salesman as intermediary** — transmit the firm's product
  and prices, absorb customer temperament, generate order flow. The
  trader's info edge (ch3/6) is exercised through the sales force.
- "Geek" = new salesman, lowest rung between trainee and man; abuse was
  the initiation.

## London and the globalization push
- London grew 150 → 900 staff during Lewis's tenure; Salomon poured tens
  of millions into going "global" (the conventional wisdom that a few
  global banks would form an oligopoly).
- Why London: time zone, language, political stability, dollar-hungry
  capital, light regulation, and no barrier to entry (unlike Japan's
  labyrinthine regulation).
- The cultural contrast: Europeans treated Gutfreund "like a gauche
  American tourist" and prioritized customer relationships over the
  latest gadgets — relationship banking vs. product-driven American
  trading.
- Twelve sales units in London were extensions of New York desks (bonds,
  mortgages, governments, equities, options/futures…).

## Key takeaways
- Sales and trading are two sides of one franchise: the trader's edge
  only monetizes through the sales force's customer relationships.
- Globalization was a growth bet built on the assumption of a coming
  oligopoly — a strategic conviction that proved costly when the 1987
  crash hit (ch11).
- Cold-calling institutional investors is the raw training for
  understanding what customers actually want — and what they'll
  tolerate.

## Notes
- Foreshadows ch9–11: the London sales operation was where Lewis sold the
  risky bonds (O&Y, Southland) that the later chapters revolve around.
- For a quant repo: the chapter documents how distribution and
  relationships, not just models, drive a trading franchise.
