# Ch04 — Adult Education

**Source:** Michael Lewis, *Liar's Poker* (1989), Chapter 4.

## Purpose
Inside the Salomon Brothers **training program** — the socialization
machine that taught recruits the firm's culture: hierarchy, fear,
discretion, and the placement game that decided careers.

## The training program as cultural indoctrination
- Trainees (mostly elite grads) were treated as the lowest caste —
  hazed, goofed on, and told they weren't fit to fetch coffee for the
  traders. The abuse was initiation ("goofs"), not just cruelty.
- **Discretion was law**: bragging to the press about money was a firing
  offense (the LA man in Newsweek was sacked). Public silence protected
  the firm's mystique.
- Job placement at the end decided everything; the firm (Jim Massey)
  assigned desks, and trainees had no say. Asking pointed questions
  (about the Arab blacklist, South African shareholders) earned black
  marks — curiosity about the firm's ethics was not welcome.
- The message: fit in, don't question, produce. "What distinguishes work
  from school: curious minds were not what Massey was after."

## The economics of the program
- Recruits were cheap, abundant (fresh M.B.A.s), and fungible: even the
  "best and brightest" were interchangeable inputs to the revenue
  machine. The firm scaled by hiring classes of hundreds.
- Rapid expansion (Salomon grew explosively in 1985) meant the firm was
  hiring faster than it could socialize — a fragility revealed in 1987
  (ch11) when the giant trainee class was decimated.

## Key takeaways
- Organizational culture is built deliberately: hierarchy, hazing, and
  secrecy align individuals to the firm's risk appetite.
- Information control (don't talk to press, don't ask about ethics) was
  part of the moat — and part of the eventual scandal exposure.
- Training programs were an assembly line for talent: cheap, replaceable,
  and optimized for conformity over questioning.

## Notes
- The chapter is sociology, but the lesson for trading organizations is
  real: culture determines risk behavior, and hiring-for-conformity
  creates groupthink that amplifies later blowups (the 1987 firings, the
  CMO-era excesses).
- Paired with ch3 (the money machine) and ch11 (the crash) it completes
  the picture of the firm's rise and fragility.
