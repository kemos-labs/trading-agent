# Ch14 — Dumb Money

**Source:** Scott Patterson, *Dark Pools* (2012), Chapter 14.

## Purpose
The revelation that defines the next era: Island is a pool of sharks.
Thomas Peterffy (Interactive Brokers/Timber Hill) explains it to
Andresen — "On Island I do not make money" — leading Island to court
**dumb money** (retail order flow), and introducing payment for order
flow as the mechanism for harvesting it.

## The shark pool
- A mysterious subscriber, IBKR (Interactive Brokers), floods Island
  with millions of shares/day, then disappears. Andresen visits
  Peterffy, the Hungarian immigrant legend who priced options on
  computers first (1970s), put handhelds on the Amex floor (1980s), and
  ran the mysterious HAL signaling device on the NYSE floor.
- Peterffy's verdict: Island is faster and cheaper, but "on Island I do
  not make money" — the traders he faces are too good: Watcher
  gunslingers, superfast hedge funds (ATD), bank desks, the first HFT
  robots. A pool full of sharks devouring one another.
- Andresen's epiphany: by being fastest/cheapest, Island attracted only
  the most sophisticated traders — pros vs. pros, zero edge. Island
  needed **dumb money**: retail investors from E\*Trade, Schwab,
  Ameritrade.

## Cost of ignorance and payment for order flow
- The economics: market makers widen quotes to protect against the
  "cost of ignorance" — trading against better-informed investors.
  Retail flow from a discount broker is uninformed (Aunt Millie, Uncle
  Joe), so it *reduces* the cost of ignorance and is profitable to
  execute against.
- Payment for order flow: brokers sell their retail flow to firms like
  Knight Securities (Jersey City), which pays for the orders and
  internalizes them — pioneered by Bernie Madoff years before.
  Ameritrade's flow went to Knight, so Andresen's speed pitch lost to
  hard dollars.
- The sharks need food: sophisticated firms acting as market makers
  starve without retail flow to feed on — the market literally evolves
  to harvest uninformed order flow.

## Renaissance
- Andresen pitches Renaissance Technologies; Jim Simons appears to doze
  off, then reels off every Island weakness (no NYSE access, no
  institutional flow, regulatory risk). Brown and Mercer (ex-IBM AI
  experts) run the automated Nova fund inside Medallion; fast flawless
  execution on Island boosts their models. The lesson: the most
  sophisticated quant funds need speed infrastructure, not cleverness
  in order routing.

## Key takeaways
- Who you're matched against determines your edge — venue composition
  matters as much as venue speed.
- Uninformed order flow is a real, tradeable resource; payment for
  order flow is how brokers monetize it.
- Retail execution quality is structurally entangled with who pays for
  the flow.
