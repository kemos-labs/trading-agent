# Prologue — Light Pool

**Source:** Scott Patterson, *Dark Pools* (2012), Prologue.

## Purpose
Sets the book's thesis: the entire U.S. stock market has become one vast
**dark pool** — orders hidden, algorithms secret, regulators and
investors in the dark. Opens at the March 10, 2011 Credit Suisse Equity
Trading Forum in Miami, where market "Plumber" Dan Mathisson unveils his
Light Pool answer to the market's opacity.

## The Plumbers
- Mathisson is an elite market **Plumber** — an architect of the pipes
  connecting markets, not of strategies. His Credit Suisse platform AES
  (Advanced Electronic Systems) matched ~14% of U.S. stock volume (~1B
  shares/day) by 2011 and earned ~$800M in 2008, the bank's most
  profitable arm. He created Guerilla, the first mass-marketed
  robot-trading algorithm designed to evade detection by other algos.
- Plumbers grew powerful because a new breed of trader games the
  plumbing itself: exploiting loopholes and quirks in the market's
  blueprints.

## The Flash Crash shadow
- The May 6, 2010 Flash Crash (Dow −800 points in minutes) revealed the
  computer-driven market as dangerous and opaque. Mutual funds saw cash
  outflows every month through 2010 even as the market rallied — retail
  investors concluded the market was rigged or unsafe.
- Mathisson's diagnosis: robot trading machines **front-run long-term
  investors**. If Fidelity tries to buy 1M IBM shares, bots detect the
  order, buy IBM first, and run up the price; on sells they push it
  down.

## Dark pools and the thesis
- Large traders flee the "lit" pools (NYSE, Nasdaq) into **dark pools** —
  private venues that mask orders from the public market. Dark pools are
  nearly unregulated and their blueprints are guarded secrets; highly
  paid Ph.D.s spend their days reverse-engineering them.
- Dark pools are both a *solution* to lit-market front-running and a
  *symptom*: the exchanges themselves feed the predators by selling
  data-rich feeds that broadcast institutional order flow to HFT firms.
- The book's central claim (footnote 1): "dark pool" narrowly means a
  venue that masks orders, but Patterson argues the **entire market has
  become one vast dark pool** — every corner hides orders, and the AI
  trading systems are cloaked in secrecy. Levine's dream of a lit,
  transparent market has inverted.

## Key takeaway
Speed, secrecy, and data exclusivity — not fundamental information —
now determine who wins. The market's plumbing is a weapon, and the
people who control it (Plumbers and their HFT clients) hold the edge.
