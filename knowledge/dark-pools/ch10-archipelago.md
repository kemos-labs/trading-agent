# Ch10 — Archipelago

**Source:** Scott Patterson, *Dark Pools* (2012), Chapter 10.

## Purpose
Jerry Putnam's journey from failed stockbroker to ECN founder: how
**Archipelago** — "a chain of islands" — created the *order-routing
conduit* model that linked the pools, and how the January 1997
Order-Handling Rules opened the door for anyone willing to fight
Nasdaq's last-minute stonewalling.

## Putnam's backstory
- Putnam (b. 1958, Wharton '81) fails his way through Dean Witter,
  PaineWebber, Prudential; in 1994, out of options, he founds broker
  dealer **Terra Nova Trading**. A chance meeting with Stuart Townsend
  (creator of RealTick, a Windows-based real-time quote system) shows
  him the software wave.
- He takes Harvey Houtkin's SOES training class (Feb 1995) — not to
  trade, but to learn the business: equipment, Nasdaq hookups, fees.
  He sets up **Chicago Trading & Arbitrage** day-trading rooms,
  connecting "trading salons" across the country via the Internet to one
  central Level II Workstation — an early remote-order-routing model.

## The Order-Handling Rules scramble
- As the rules near, Nasdaq raises its ECN account requirement from
  $250k to $1M — nearly killing Putnam. A $750k loan from clearing firm
  Southwest Securities (for 5% of the company) saves him. Rules go live
  Jan 20, 1997; Archipelago's quotes appear alongside Island, Instinet,
  and Bloomberg Tradebook.
- Putnam asks Nasdaq's own presentation whether Nasdaq will build its
  own ECN; the answer is no — Nasdaq is protecting its market makers.
  That inaction hands the market to the upstarts.

## The Archipelago model
- Putnam's innovation: Archipelago wouldn't only match internally — it
  would **route orders to whatever pool had the match** (Nasdaq, Island,
  Instinet), tapping the volume of the *entire* market. An archipelago
  of linked pools instead of an isolated island.
- Dec 27, 1996: Putnam and Townsend found Archipelago Holdings (50/50).
  Levine — who saw Putnam as an ally against the establishment — helps
  him, even emailing Island code and building a direct Island↔Archipelago
  link, bypassing Nasdaq's antiquated plumbing.

## Key takeaways
- In fragmented markets, the routing layer (smart order routing) can be
  as valuable as the matching engine.
- Capital requirements and incumbents' games are the real barriers to
  entry in market infrastructure — regulators' indifference to building
  their own ECN left Nasdaq vulnerable.
- Collaboration between "competitors" (Levine & Putnam) against a
  common incumbent shaped the future market.
