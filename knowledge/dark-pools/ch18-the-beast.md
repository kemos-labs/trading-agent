# Ch18 — The Beast

**Source:** Scott Patterson, *Dark Pools* (2012), Chapter 18.

## Purpose
The absorption of the ECNs by the incumbents: the NYSE–Archipelago
merger (April 20, 2005) and Nasdaq's purchase of the Island engine
($935M), driven by **Reg NMS** — the rule that decreed speed mattered
more than price and forced the big exchanges to buy the technology they
had fought.

## NYSE–Archipelago
- Announced April 20, 2005; NYSE Group becomes a for-profit public
  company (ticker NYX), IPO March 6, 2006 at ~$10B market cap. The
  1,366 seat-holders get ~$2M each. Putnam negotiates for ~1/3 for
  Archipelago shareholders; Thain keeps 70% for NYSE stakeholders.
  Goldman is on both sides (15% of Archipelago + seats via Spear,
  Leeds) — traders nickname it "the Goldman Sachs Exchange."
- The NYSE's technology is a Rube Goldberg machine: DOT orders printed
  on paper and matched by typing staff ("this was 2005"); software from
  the Black Monday era. Putnam's team integrates Arca's electronic
  system; the floor becomes a prop for TV.

## Nasdaq buys Island
- Nasdaq pays $935M for Instinet's trading engine — the Island system —
  championed by Chris Concannon (ex-Island attorney, now Nasdaq EVP),
  whose goal was always to take out the NYSE. SuperMontage is scrapped;
  Nasdaq migrates all trading to the Island engine (INET). Chi-X Europe
  (Nigito's Java rewrite) later takes >25% of European volume.

## Reg NMS
- Reg NMS (passed early 2005, fully implemented summer 2007) mandates
  that orders route to the venue with the **best price** — the
  "trade-through" rule — and, critically, allows electronic venues to
  bypass slow manual markets entirely: speed is effectively prioritized
  over price for manual floors. The 10–20 second NYSE floor execution
  was untenable; Reg NMS makes it legal to ignore.
- Consequences: HFT can finally trade NYSE-listed names; Getco's
  earnings jump from ~$50M (2006) to ~$9M/day (2007); Citadel's Tactical
  Trading (run by Misha Malyshev) pulls in $1B in 2008. 2007–08 is the
  Bots' heyday.
- Cummings founds **BATS** (Better Alternative Trading System) to
  pressure the merged giants; Knight renames Attain to **Direct Edge**.
  BATS mimics Island (its feed is called FAST PITCH, after ITCH) and
  takes >10% of volume by 2007.

## Key takeaways
- Regulation that mandates best-price routing + speed precedence forces
  incumbents to adopt the disruptors' technology — the revolution is
  completed by acquisition.
- Market-structure rules (Reg NMS) can be more consequential than any
  trading strategy: they decide *where* liquidity lives.
- The HFT "arms race" reshapes exchange business models: exchanges now
  sell speed and data to the same traders they host.
