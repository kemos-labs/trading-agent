# Ch11 — Everyone Cares

**Source:** Scott Patterson, *Dark Pools* (2012), Chapter 11.

## Purpose
Island professionalizes and invents **maker-taker** (June 1, 1998) —
paying firms to provide liquidity. The chapter covers the momentum
day-trading boom (CyberTrader, dot-com mania), Island's first hires
(Will Sterling, Matt Andresen), and the perverse consequence Levine
later regrets: a new class of middlemen — the rebate traders.

## The momentum boom
- CyberTrader (built by Philip Berber out of Houston's Block Trading;
  sold to Charles Schwab for ~$500M in 2000) turns day trading into a
  mass phenomenon. Unlike SOES scalpers (flat overnight), "mo-mo"
  traders rode positions for days, leveraged, pushing dot-com volatility
  into the stratosphere.

## Island's team
- Will Sterling (19, University of Texas dropout) becomes Island's first
  full-time hire after developing CyberTrader. Matt Andresen — Duke
  fencer, ex-Lehman, SOES ace at Datek — is made CEO in late 1997.
  Levine: "You're a smart person who cares." "Everyone cares." "No, they
  don't." Andresen hires programmers like Mike Lazarev on the spot ("Fuck
  that! Forget the process!").
- Levine remains the anonymous genius — gold bars left on desks, no
  title, no management interest.

## Maker-taker
- Island charged $1/order with no scale incentive; big traders (ATD,
  Renaissance) wanted cheaper. Levine, re-reading Schwartz's chapter on
  the **cost of ignorance** (the cost of trading against better-informed
  investors), inverts it: *pay* firms to post liquidity.
- June 1, 1998: Island pays 1¢ per 100 shares for liquidity **makers**;
  **takers** pay 2½¢ per 100 shares; Island keeps the 1½¢ difference.
  Patience is rewarded; whoever crosses the spread pays.
- Tiny per-trade, huge at scale: rebate capture becomes a strategy in
  its own right — "rebate traders" race to the front of the queue, snap
  the rebate, pivot, repeat. Liquidity breeds liquidity; nearly every
  pool copies the model, and maker-taker comes to dominate the market.
- The irony: Island was built to eliminate middlemen; maker-taker
  created a new class of them (HFT). Levine's colleagues: "We created a
  monster."

## Key takeaways
- Incentive design (paying for liquidity) reshapes order-flow behavior
  — market structure is a policy instrument.
- The make/take fee asymmetry is the economic engine of modern
  exchanges; understand it before trading.
- Speed + volume + rebates is a viable standalone strategy — and the
  seed of the toxic-order-types era (ch24).
