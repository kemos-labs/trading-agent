# Ch17 — "I Do Not Want to Be a Famous Person"

**Source:** Scott Patterson, *Dark Pools* (2012), Chapter 17.

## Purpose
The reckoning and fade-out of the Datek generation: the Instinet–Island
merger's dysfunction, the SEC's January 2003 fraud settlement against
Datek's founders, and Levine's unheralded departure — plus his failed
attempt to rebuild the market's data backbone (the Big J SIP) for $1.

## The merger and the exit of the bandits
- The Instinet–Island merger (Oct 2002) is a godsend for Archipelago:
  Instinet is a bloated tech dinosaur, and its CEO Ed Nicoll is buried
  in layoffs. Island's talent drains away — Andresen to Sanford C.
  Bernstein (then Citadel), Sterling to UBS; Java expert Brian Nigito
  (later Citadel/Getco) joins and eventually rewrites the engine,
  spawning Chi-X Europe.
- Jan 14, 2003: the SEC settles its decade-long Datek case — ~$70M in
  fines for illegal trading and fraudulent bookkeeping (the Wire's
  retroactive trade allocation). Citron pays $22.5M + $29.2M and is
  barred from the securities industry forever; Maschler is fined and
  retires to Boca; Levine pays $1M for bookkeeping fraud but isn't
  barred. Citron later co-founds Vonage.

## Levine's exit and the Big J SIP
- Levine hates Instinet's bureaucracy and leaves quietly — no press
  release; colleagues simply notice he's gone. He pursues wind power
  and LawDact (automated legal documents).
- His last big idea: rebuild the **SIP** (the slow Securities
  Information Processor that HFT firms arbitrage) — the **Big J SIP**
  — for **$1**, running it for a year as proof, then putting it in a
  nonprofit trust; technology costs <$100K/year. Endorsed by ATD's Dave
  Whitcomb and Jamie Selway, it is nonetheless rejected — the SEC's
  stain on Levine made him untouchable — and the committee pays >$10M
  to another firm instead. The market keeps its slow, exploitable SIP.
- Levine's 2011 verdict on the race to zero: "people spending millions
  (billions?) of dollars to save milliseconds... What an expensive and
  needless mess."

## Key takeaways
- Trust and regulatory standing are capital in market infrastructure —
  the SEC's case made Levine's low-cost public-good offer unpalatable.
- Infrastructure that stays slow (the SIP) becomes a standing arbitrage
  opportunity for the fast.
- The revolution's architect avoided fame by design ("Any story about
  Island should not include a picture of me"), letting the traders and
  executives take credit — and the blame.
