# Ch13 — Brownian Motion

**Source:** *Stochastic Calculus and Finance* (1997 lecture notes, Part II)
— Steven Shreve

## From random walk to Brownian motion

Start with the symmetric random walk M_k = Σ X_j (X_j = ±1). Scale space by
1/√k and time by k: M_k/√k is standard normal as k → ∞ (CLT). Taking the
continuous limit of the scaled walk (with step size → 0) gives Brownian
motion W(t), also called the Wiener process.

## Definition and properties

W(t) is Brownian motion on (Ω, F, P) if:
1. W(0) = 0 a.s.; paths are **continuous** functions of t.
2. Independent increments: for 0 = t₀ ≤ t₁ ≤ ... ≤ t_n, the increments
   W(t_{k+1}) − W(t_k) are independent.
3. Normal increments: W(t_{k+1}) − W(t_k) ~ N(0, t_{k+1} − t_k) — mean 0,
   variance = elapsed time.

Immediate consequences:
- **Covariance**: Cov(W(s), W(t)) = min(s, t).
- **Finite-dimensional distributions** are joint normal (it's a Gaussian
  process, ch29).
- **Filtration**: F(t) = σ(W(u), 0 ≤ u ≤ t) — the information generated by
  the path up to t. W(t) is adapted; future increments are independent of
  F(t).
- **Martingale property**: W(t) is a martingale (E[W(t)|F(s)] = W(s)).
- **Markov property**: future depends on the path only through the present
  value (transition density p(t; x, y) = normal density).
- **First passage time**: the distribution of τ = min{t : W(t) = x} comes
  from the reflection principle (ch20/25).

## The limit of the binomial model

The binomial model converges to Brownian motion: with u = e^{σ√Δt},
d = e^{−σ√Δt}, and the right scaling of probabilities (r ≈ 0 or the
risk-neutral twist), log S_k = log S₀ + σ·(random walk). The limit is
**geometric Brownian motion** dS = μS dt + σS dW — the Black-Scholes
underlying.

## Key facts for trading

- **Quadratic variation** [W](t) = t (limit of Σ(ΔW)²): Brownian motion
  accumulates variance at rate 1 per unit time — this is why σ² drives
  option pricing and why paths are nowhere differentiable.
- Infinite first variation: paths wiggle so much the total variation is
  infinite (⇒ classical calculus fails; need Itô calculus).
- Starting at points other than 0, time changes, and multi-dimensional
  versions (correlated Brownian motions) all extend the definition.

## Key takeaways

- Brownian motion: continuous paths, independent normal increments with
  variance = time — the scaling limit of the random walk and the binomial
  model.
- It is a martingale, Markov, Gaussian; covariance min(s,t).
- Quadratic variation = t (and σ²t for σ·W): the engine of Itô calculus
  and of Black-Scholes (σ² is the rate at which variance accumulates).
- Paths are continuous but nowhere differentiable — Riemann-Stieltjes
  integration fails, motivating the Itô integral (ch14).
